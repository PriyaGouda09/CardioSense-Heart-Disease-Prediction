Abstract—The early detection and rapid identification of 
life-threatening diseases play crucial roles in saving lives. 
However, disparities in healthcare infrastructure and the 
unequal distribution of medical resources often hinder timely 
diagnosis, resulting in adverse outcomes. This study introduces 
a Multi-Disease Prediction System, a user-friendly web 
application developed with Streamlit, capable of predicting 
kidney disease, breast cancer, and heart disease. Unlike 
traditional AI models that focus on predicting a single disease, 
our objective is to develop a unified platform using Streamlit 
and 
Machine 
learning 
to 
predict 
multiple 
diseases 
simultaneously. The system harnesses advanced machine 
learning techniques, utilizing algorithms such as the Random 
Forest, Gradient Boosting Classifier, XGBoost, K-Nearest 
Neighbor, and Decision Tree classifier. For each disease, the 
optimal algorithm was selected based on its performance, 
yielding predictive accuracies of 99% for kidney disease 
(Random Forest Classifier), 98% for breast cancer (XGBoost), 
and 95% for heart disease (Random Forest Classifier). Users 
can input health indicators and symptoms and receive 
personalized risk insights. By offering an intuitive and 
interactive platform for data-driven screening, this application 
seeks to enhance early detection and encourage proactive 
health management in diverse populations. These models are 
designed to continuously improve through advancements in 
machine learning, ensuring reliable and impactful health 
predictions for broader accessibility and preventive care. 
Additionally, future improvements, such as integrating more 
diseases into the system, would further enhance its capabilities 
and expand its impact on early disease detection.

I. INTRODUCTION  
 
Global health faces substantial challenges from long-term 
illnesses, such as kidney disease, breast cancer, and heart 
disease. These chronic conditions significantly contribute to 
the worldwide burden of death and illness. Early detection 
and intervention are critical to effectively managing these 
conditions, as timely diagnosis can improve patient outcomes 
and quality of life while reducing the long-term burden on 
healthcare systems. However, conventional diagnostic 
methods often require specialized facilities and in-person 
consultations, which may not be accessible to all individuals, 
particularly in underserved regions. This study leveraged 
machine learning (ML) to build predictive models for kidney 
disease, breast cancer, and heart disease, with the aim of 
developing a unified and accessible platform for risk 
assessment and early disease detection. Using the Streamlit 
Python framework, the project introduced an interactive web 
interface that provides users with an intuitive and practical 
tool for personalized health management. 
A variety of advanced machine learning techniques, 
including Random Forest, Decision Tree Classifier, K-
Nearest Neighbor method, Gradient Boosting Classifier, and 
XGboost, are integrated into the suggested system to analyze 
patient data and produce predictions. The system ensures 
high reliability of its predictions by selecting the most 
accurate model for each disease. These results underscore the 
potential of the system to make precise and reliable disease 
predictions across multiple conditions, thereby providing 
users with valuable insights into their health. The user 
interface, built on the StreamLit platform, makes disease 
prediction accessible to a broad audience, including 
individuals without technical expertise.  
Furthermore, this project tackles healthcare access 
challenges by providing a remote, data-driven diagnostic tool 
accessible from any location, reducing dependence on 
traditional 
healthcare 
infrastructure. 
By 
enhancing 
accessibility, especially in resource-limited regions, this 
system brings disease prediction closer to underserved 
populations. Through advanced machine learning techniques, 
the project exemplifies how technology can transform 
preventive healthcare, helping bridge gaps in healthcare 
access. It not only supports early diagnosis but also promotes 
health awareness, encouraging proactive health management. 
Overall, this innovative digital health solution showcases the 
potential of machine learning to improve patient outcomes 
and redefine healthcare accessibility.

B. Data Preprocessing 
Data preparation is the process of converting raw data 
into an understandable format by reduction, transformation, 
and cleaning procedures. Necessary missing data points 
received imputation through mean and mode substitution for 
variables that were continuous or categorical respectively. 
The LabelEncoder() function from sklearn.preprocessing 
encoded all categorical features for this application. The 
analysis included an Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) to 
show data distributions as well as discover patterns of 
missing information and locate outliers and measure 
correlations between features. The analysis used correlation 
matrices to detect features which showed excessive 
correlation therefore they were eliminated. SMOTE enabled 
us to handle class imbalance by generating synthetic 
minority examples which provided balanced training 
conditions. 
C. Splitting Train-Test 
The dataset went through preprocessing then split into 
training (80%) along with testing (20%) sections through 
train_test_split() with stratification setting the value as Y for 
keeping class distributions balanced. A model validation 
through k-fold cross-validation (k=5) was conducted to 
prevent overfitting and enhance generalization. 
D. Model Training  
After splitting our dataset, we first use the training data to 
train our model using a variety of machine learning 
techniques and classifiers. For any prediction system in our 
situation, we use the Random Forest, Decision Tree 
Classifier, k-nearest neighbor algorithm, Gradient Boosting 
Classifier and XGboost techniques to train the model. 
Hyperparameter tuning was performed using GridSearchCV 
optimizing parameters like learning rate, depth, and 
estimators to obtain better accuracy levels. 
E. Model Evaluation and Optimal Model Selection 
Model performance is evaluated using parameters such as
 accuracy, precision, recall, and F1score to ensure accurate a
nd robust results. The best model is chosen after its accuracy 
is compared to other models. We proceed to Model 
Prediction, where the model is tested for custom inputs, 
using the best machine learning method and the testing data. 
F. Model Deployment 
After saving each model, we use Pickle to load them into 
our web app's database. Finally, we use "streamlit" to 
develop the web app. and "streamlit-options-menu", and then 
install our model. We use constraint checks, such as drop-
down columns and error pop-ups, to guarantee that users 
provide consistent data to our models.

D. Web-App Stimulation Model Deployment 
Our multi-disease forecasting system has its web-based 
interface built through "streamlit" Python library deployment 
for optimized user experience. To access multiple web pages, 
we incorporated the "streamlit options-menu" library into our 
program. Each webpage includes specific prediction 
algorithm titles while the input areas function as attribute 
names. The system indicates the "prediction result" button 
for users to submit their test requests after completing 
required inputs. In specific predictive models the input field 
type ambiguity between integer, floating-point or string 
values gets validated. These systems present pre-text 
guidance to users immediately before the input fields and 
dropdown menus which contain required entry areas. 
V. CONCUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE 
The proposed Multi-Disease Prediction System shows 
computer learning methods that improve healthcare 
accessibility by identifying diseases early. The system 
leverages advanced algorithms along with a user-friendly 
web application that Streamlit created to deliver immediate 
accurate evaluations of kidney disease and breast cancer and 
heart disease. The platform completes missing capabilities 
found in typical diagnostic approaches through which users 
specifically in underserved regions can obtain meaningful 
health data. The integration of several diseases into one 
framework improves its practical value while increasing 
operational efficiency thus providing a major advancement 
for data-driven accessible healthcare solutions. The system 
has the potential for transformation in preventive health 
management due to ongoing development activities along 
with user feedback implementation. 
Future Advancements: 
• 
In order to improve generalizability and lessen 
biases, future iterations of the system will use larger, 
more diverse datasets from several sources. 
• 
For more reliable predictions, use deep learning 
methods like Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) 
and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) to evaluate 
complicated data, such as time-series data and 
medical imaging. 
• 
To further improve accessibility and allow for on-
the-go health assessments, develop a mobile 
application version of the system. 
• 
To ensure inclusivity and serve a varied user base 
worldwide, include support for many languages.
