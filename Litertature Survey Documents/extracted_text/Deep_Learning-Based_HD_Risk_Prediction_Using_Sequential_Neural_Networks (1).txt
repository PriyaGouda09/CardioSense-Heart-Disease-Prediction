Abstract—Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) continue to be 
the leading cause of global death, requiring accurate and 
efficient prediction technology to aid clinicians in their 
decision-making.  Conventional diagnostic methods, 
such as ECG or stress tests, need manual assessment and 
may lead to diagnostic delays or inconsistencies.  Recent 
breakthroughs in AI and deep learning have potential 
for computer-automated risk prediction models that 
alleviate diagnostic uncertainties.  This study presents a 
deep learning methodology (Sequential ANN) for 
forecasting HD.  A dataset on Heart Disease (HD), 
containing 14 clinical factors such as sex, age, fasting 
blood sugar resting blood pressure, cholesterol levels 
and chest pain type, is employed for model training.  The 
trained artificial neural network utilizes the Adam 
optimizer (AO) and binary cross-entropy loss (BCEL), 
leading to swift convergence and improved classification 
accuracy.  The experimental results demonstrate that 
the predictive performance is outstanding, with the 
model attaining an overall performance of 98% 
(Precision: 98.01%, Recall: 98%, F1 score: 98%, ROC 
AUC: 97.77%).  Experimental results demonstrate the 
effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed method for 
early cardiac disease prediction, applicable in real-time 
risk assessment and clinical decision support systems.

RELATED WORK 
Mondal, S. A et al. [5] (2025) study introduces an ANN-
based intelligent healthcare system for predicting CHD risk, 
utilizing advanced preprocessing and sampling techniques 
to address class imbalance. Experimental results indicate 
that attaining high validation accuracy and recall value is 
more desirable, suggesting the model's potential as a 
dependable decision support tool. Nonetheless, numerous 
constraints exist: reliance on a single dataset, restricted 
generalization, and the necessity for real-world clinical 
validation prior to adoption. 
Lilhore, U. K et al. [6] (2025) proposed work introduces 
a MMAM with Deep BiLSTM (M2AM with Deep 
BiLSTM) to enhance HD classification through the dynamic 
selection of class-specific attention weights. The results 
demonstrate higher performance in accuracy, precision, 
recall, and F-measure, greatly surpassing conventional 
ML/DL techniques, while also reducing noise and 
misclassification. 
Nonetheless, 
the 
attention-based 
architecture is intricate and relies on computationally 
intensive preparation procedures for acquiring data 
representations that can be efficiently extended to our varied 
real-world datasets. 
Kalatehjari, E et al. [7] (2024) work presents an EOA-
enhanced 
LSTM 
(O-SBGC-LSTM) 
that 
effectively 
integrates spatial-temporal correlations for the early 
prevention and detection of diabetes. Experimental results 
indicate that the suggested model achieves superior 
performance, with an accuracy over 98%, surpassing 
conventional machine learning and hybrid DL models 
through the integration of fuzzy-based inference for 
preventive 
recommendations. 
Limitations 
encompass 
significant model complexity and the necessity for vast 
datasets to adequately train the systems, along with a need 
for further broad clinical validation proof of concepts. 
 Kalatehjari, E. et al. [8] (2025) study introduces a CNN-
BiLSTM fusion model that utilizes both ECG and PCG 
inputs to improve the diagnosis and risk classification of 
CAD patients. The findings indicate that the bilinear fusion 
of CNN and BiLSTM predictions achieves a diagnosis 
accuracy of 97% on the PhysioNet CinC 2016 dataset, 
surpassing models trained with single signals or other 
prevalent competitors. Nonetheless, constraints encompass 
dependence on a singular benchmark dataset, heightened 
computing expenses for ensemble-based architectures, and 
the necessity for comprehensive real-world clinical 
validation. 
Yu, J et . al [9] (2025) study utilizes a longitudinal deep 
learning model, Dynamic-DeepHit, to predict ten-year 
ASCVD risk by employing eight years of repeated risk 
factor data, rather than the conventional cross-sectional PCE 
approach. Outcomes the deep learning model surpassed 
PCE anyway. SOFA attained an AUROC of 0.815, 
surpassing 0.792, with comparable calibration and a net 
reclassification score of 0.385, highlighting the enhanced 
value derived from incorporating predictions of longitudinal 
risk factors. Caveats encompass the utilization of cohort-
based data, possible computational expenses for drug 
development applications, and the necessity for validation in 
external cohorts comprising a broader patient demographic.

A. 
Dataset Acquisition and Understanding 
This study used a cardiovascular disease dataset of 14 
clinical characteristics. One aspect pertains to demographic 
variables such as sex and age ,while the other encompasses 
diagnostic factors including maximum heart rate achieved,  
cholesterol level, resting ECG results,  resting blood 
pressure, exercise-induced angina, fasting blood sugar, chest 
pain type, slope (slope of the peak ST segment during 
exercise), oldpeak (ST depression induced by exercise 
relative to rest)and the number of major vessels [11]. The 
goal classes are 0 and 1, denoting the absence and presence 
of cardiac disease, respectively. The dataset comprises 
diverse category, binary, and numeric variables, rendering it 
suitable for (ML) and (DL) classification applications. 
Features are articulated in medical units, ensuring that 
forecasts are therapeutically relevant. This diversity of 
features provides a robust foundation for training models to 
identify 
patterns 
potentially 
associated 
with 
urban 
cardiovascular risk [12]. 
B. Data Preprocessing 
Preprocessing was performed before the modeling phase 
to remove data outliers. Polygons with missing or altered 
values were treated accordingly to prevent bias in the 
learning 
process. 
Numerical 
properties 
have 
been 
normalized by min-max scaling to ensure all variables are 
within a uniform range. Categorical features, such as chest 
pain kind, resting ECG, slope of ST segment, and number of 
main vessels, were converted into numerical format using 
label encoding. Categorical variables, such as fasting blood 
sugar, 
gender, 
and 
exercise-induced 
angina, 
were 
maintained in their binary form. The dataset was randomly 
mixed to eliminate any ordering effects and thereafter 
divided into training and testing sets for equitable 
comparison. These measures were essential for maintaining 
a clean, consistent, and well-structured dataset for effective 
deep learning [13]. 
C. Model Architecture 
The predictor is a sequential artificial neural network for 
binary classification. The network initiates with an input 
layer that processes 13 inputs supplied by the dataset. We 
incorporated an additional hidden layer, consisting of a fully 
linked layer with a ReLU activation function, enabling the 
learning of more intricate non-linear patterns at the input 
stage. 
Dropout 
layers 
are 
employed 
to 
enhance 
generalization and prevent overfitting. The output layer 
employs the sigmoid activation function to provide 
probabilities ranging from 0 to 1 for heart illness. The model 
employs an AO that determines distinct learning rates for 
each feature dimension based on the previous gradient, with 
a BCEL appropriate for binary classification. This system is 
intricate yet effectively balances strength and efficiency in 
risk distribution, ensuring consistent performance in risk 
type categorization [14]. 
D. Model Training and Optimization 
The 
artificial 
neural 
network 
was 
trained 
on 
preprocessed data utilizing a supervised learning method. 
The model underwent extensive training over numerous 
epochs, 
with 
simultaneous 
modification 
of 
the 
corresponding weights to reduce classification error. We 
utilize mini-batch gradient descent to enhance model 
stability 
and 
decrease 
computational 
expenses. 
We 
employed the Adam method for optimization, which 
enhances learning rate adaptability and facilitates expedited 
convergence [15]. We employed BCEL during training to 
mitigate erroneous predictions. The entire dataset was 
randomly divided into training and testing subsets during 
the training process to evaluate performance and prevent 
overfitting. The accuracy and loss functions over the epochs 
were graphed, demonstrating that both curves converged 
without exhibiting signs of instability. These were several 
factors contributing to the success of ANN in classification 
[16]. 
E. Performance Evaluation 
 
The model was evaluated after training with a testing 
dataset. To assess performance, we construct a confusion 
matrix comprising (FP),(TP), (FN) and (TN).  Standard 
evaluation criteria, including accuracy, recall (sensitivity), 
precision, F1-score, and ROC AUC, are employed to offer a 
comprehensive assessment of the model's performance [17]. 
The model demonstrated exceptional performance across all 
evaluation metrics, indicating a strong capability for 
forecasting cardiac morbidity. Accuracy/loss curves and 
receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were 
employed 
to 
illustrate 
the 
learning 
behavior 
and 
classification efficacy. The ANN is capable of processing 
both linear and nonlinear data patterns, as indicated in this 
observation [18]. 
F. Prediction and Decision Support 
 
The novel model assessed the risk of cardiovascular 
illness in new patients. For each input case, the model 
generates a likelihood score to predict cardiovascular 
disease (CVD). A cutoff value is established to distinguish 
between those with the condition and those without it [19]. 
The suggested methodology is demonstrated by case studies 
that effectively differentiate between low- and high-risk 
experimental units with notable accuracy and confidence. 
The suggested system provides a decision assistance tool for 
physicians that substantially decreases dispensing time 
while ensuring more consistent risk assessments. Employing 
clinical characteristics and deep learning facilitates early 
diagnosis for intervention, perhaps resulting in improved 
patient outcomes [20].

V. 
CONCLUSION 
 
This study has demonstrated that the sequential ANN is 
optimal for accurately and reliably predicting the risk of 
HD. 
Utilizing 
13 
clinical 
features 
and 
extensive 
preprocessing, the model successfully identified both linear 
and nonlinear associations within the data. The performance 
evaluation yielded outstanding results across several criteria, 
hence validating the model's appropriateness for clinical 
applications. The case studies also validated its capacity to 
accurately differentiate between low- and high-risk patients. 
In conclusion, the suggested framework offers a pragmatic 
decision support instrument for early diagnosis and 
healthcare prevention. In the future, the model may be 
expanded and generalized utilizing bigger, well-diversified 
datasets from single-center research, employing the XAI 
technique in conjunction with large-scale wearable IoT 
devices for continuous real-time patient monitoring.
