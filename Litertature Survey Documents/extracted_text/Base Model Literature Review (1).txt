Literature Review for Selection of Base Model 
Heart disease remains one of the leading causes of global mortality, demanding accurate and 
early prediction systems. With the advancement of Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning 
(DL), predictive models have significantly improved diagnostic accuracy and decision support 
systems in cardiology. 
Recent studies show that traditional classifiers such as Support Vector Machine (SVM) and 
Random Forest (RF) remain widely used due to their robustness and interpretability [8]. 
However, more advanced approaches such as Stacked Ensemble Learning [1], Blending-based 
Networks [5], and Deep Learning with Transfer Learning [3] have demonstrated superior 
performance in certain datasets. 
Binary classification models generally achieve higher accuracy compared to multiclass 
prediction models, which remain more complex and challenging [1], [6]. Furthermore, 
preprocessing techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and optimized feature 
selection have been shown to significantly improve model performance [2]. 
This review analyzes and compares major machine learning techniques used for heart disease 
prediction to identify suitable base models for further experimentation. 
Research Gaps Indetified 
Based on the reviewed literature, the following research gaps are observed: 
1.​ Lower Performance in Multiclass Classification – Multiclass models show 
significantly lower accuracy compared to binary classification models [1], [6].​
 
2.​ Dependence on Dataset Quality – Model performance varies across datasets such as 
Cleveland, UCI, and regional datasets, indicating limited generalizability [4], [8].​
 
3.​ Limited Benchmarking Across Models – Many studies compare limited algorithms 
rather than performing extensive benchmarking [8].​
 
4.​ Need for Dimensionality Reduction – High-dimensional medical datasets require 
preprocessing techniques like PCA for better performance [2].​
 
5.​ Ensemble Complexity – Although stacking and blending improve accuracy, they 
increase model complexity and computational cost [1], [5].​
 
6.​ Limited Explainability and Real-World Deployment – Few models integrate 
explainable AI or deploy practical web-based systems [9].​
 
These gaps suggest that starting with strong baseline models before moving to complex 
ensemble architectures is a systematic and practical approach.

Conclusion: From the literature analysis: 
●​ SVM is consistently used across multiple studies and performs reliably across datasets 
[1], [2], [4], [8].​
 
●​ MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron), as part of deep learning approaches, shows strong 
potential in learning complex nonlinear relationships [3].​
 
●​ Random Forest is robust, interpretable, and performs well in multiple comparative studies 
[1], [8], [9].​
 
Although ensemble and stacking models achieve higher accuracy [1], [5], [6], it is 
methodologically sound to first establish strong baseline models before implementing complex 
architectures. 
Therefore, for baseline models, we propose: 
1.​ Support Vector Machine (SVM)​
 
2.​ Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)​
 
3.​ Random Forest (RF)
