ABSTRACT Cardiovascular disorders are the primary cause of death on a global scale. The World Health
Organization report indicates that approximately 18 million people die from CVD each year. Major cardiac
risks include arrhythmia and coronary artery disease, among others. Recent advancements in Artificial
Intelligence play a pivotal role for life-saving interventions in CVD treatment. This survey examines the
latest progress in Machine Learning, Deep Learning and Pre-trained transfer learning models for classifying
and predicting CVD using a survey of 159 articles, including 36 image datasets, 41 signal data and
52 clinical data from various sources. The survey investigates cardiac risk factors, cardiac dysfunction
classification, various modalities and medical image processing techniques, performance metrics and hybrid
techniques. Surveys on traditional neural networks such as Convolutional Neural Networks, Artificial Neural
Networks, and Recurrent Neural Networks often achieve an accuracy rate of 70% to 95%. Leveraging
pre-trained architectures such as ResNet, DenseNet, AlexNet, MobileNet, EfficientNet and GoogLeNet,
transfer learning models consistently outperform other approaches frequently achieving accuracy levels
greater than 96%. Researchers utilize various hybrid optimization algorithms to improve overall accuracy
rate. The outcome of the survey supports a precise prognosis for patients with comorbidities. The survey
findings indicate limitations in the incorporation of multimodal data for real-time risk assessment. The study
results possess the capacity to bridge gaps in research on cardiovascular disease prediction, thereby assisting
medical practitioners in early identification and subsequent prognosis.

D. RISK FACTORS FOR CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE
1) HEART FAILURE
Heart failure occurs when the heart fails to pump sufficient
blood, causing signs such as shortness of breath, fatigue, and
swelling Ponikowski et al. [13]. Cardiologists classify heart
failure into diastolic and systolic types. Diastolic heart failure
is indeed linked to ventricular wall thickening, which limits
the heart’s diastolic function, and systolic cardiac failure is
associated with ventricular dilatation and poor pumping func-
tion Genet et al. [14]. Heart failure symptoms vary depending
on which side of the heart is affected. Left-side failure affects
blood flow to the brain and body, while right-side failure
affects blood flow to the lungs (Jessup and Brozena [15];
Lorell and Carabello [16]; Haddad et al. [17].
2) STROKE
A stroke is a severe brain ailment caused by interrupted blood
flow to the Central Nervous System (CNS) Mir et al. [18].
Strokes can be categorized into two types, including Ischemic
stroke and hemorrhagic stroke. Cerebral ischemia is caused
by blocked blood arteries (Fan et al. [19]; Perna and Temple
[20]. Hemorrhagic strokes cause brain irritation due to blood
vessel bleeds Perna and Temple [20]. Transient Ischemic
Attacks (TIA) is a brief neurological problem caused by
temporary blood flow blockage to the brain Stanciu et al. [21].
3) HEART VALVE DISEASE
Heart valve disease can affect one or more of the heart’s
valves and disrupt blood flow by affecting the aortic, mitral,
tricuspid, and pulmonary valves. Aortic valve is the last valve
that oxygenated blood passes before leaving the heart. Mitral
valve disease disrupts blood flow due to malfunctioning flaps
between the left atrium, and tricuspid valve disease disrupts
blood flow between the right atrium and ventricle Maeder et
al. [22]; Turi [23]; Shah and Raney [24]. The pulmonary valve
regulates blood circulation from the right heart to the lungs
Naeije [25].
4) HYPERTENSION
Hypertension is a primary CVD risk factor that can cause
damage to the heart, blood vessels, and organs. Primary
hypertension is common, while secondary hypertension can
be caused by underlying ailments, such as kidney disease and
sleep apnea. White coat hypertension is induced by anxiety
in the medical environment
Schrader and Middeke [26].
Resistant hypertension is difficult to control with medication,
even with three different types of drugs, including a diuretic
Calhoun et al. [27]. Isolated systolic hypertension is prevalent
among the elderly population Amery et al. [28]. Masked
hypertension is detected by ambulatory blood pressure mon-
itoring, which is conducted outside of the clinical setting
Pickering et al. [29].

A. MEDICAL IMAGE PROCESSING FOR CVD PREDICTION
A Medical image processing system for the prediction of
CVD is shown in Fig. 2. The process begins with the CVD
image data source, which covers chest pain, obesity, fainting,
shortness of breath, and fatigue. The CVD Scan report utilizes
different imaging modalities, including MRI Soni et al. [46],
PET Acharya et al. [47], along with cardiac stress tests.
To diagnose CVD, different techniques are applied, includ-
ing localization and segmentation. Localization identifies the
area of interest in medical images, while segmentation clas-
sifies images into different sections Ari et al. [48]. Once
segmented, features or irregularities within these segmented
regions can be identified.
Feature extraction is a process that reduces data by con-
verting original features into a smaller number of unique and
useful features Dougherty [49]; Haq et al. [50]. Techniques
such as relief, mRMR, LASSO and genetic algorithms can be
used to select key features Masih et al. [51]. To enhance the
data quality for early-stage coronary heart disease prediction,
researchers have applied binning and z-score methods to
address the inconsistencies of the acquired dataset.
Kalykakis et al. [153] explores to enhance the identifi-
cation and characterization of significant coronary steno-
sis using Coronary Computed Tomography Angiography
(CTCA). Hu et al. [154]. predicted a non-invasive technique
for forecasting calcified plaques in coronary arteries using CT
radiomics. The researchers analyzed radiomic features from
perivascular fat CT images and integrated them with clinical
risk factors to train and assess multiple machine learning
models.
Kolossváry et al. [155] analyzed radiomic images to
identify napkin-ring sign (NRS) plaques, which are key
indicators of major adverse cardiac events. Through the
analysis of quantitative features derived from CT images,
major variations were identified between NRS and non-
NRS plaques, proving the superiority of radiomics over
traditional approaches. Militello et al. [156] examined the
potential of radiomic features from pericoronaric adipose
tissue surrounding the anterior interventricular artery (IVA)
in evaluating coronary artery illnesses, in contrast to relying
only on clinical risk factors.

10) GENETIC ALGORITHM
Genetic algorithms (GAs) utilize natural selection to develop
predicative systems for cardiac risk. GAs iteratively improve
models through processes of selection, crossover, and muta-
tion, similar to how genes change over generations. By com-

11) MULTI-LAYER PERCEPTRON
MLP, a type of Artificial Neural Network (ANN), processes

with hidden layers using non-linear activation functions to
extract features from the data. Based on the learned pat-
terns from the training data, the network classifies ECG data
as either high or low risk for cardiac disease Kumari and
Kumar [70].

PREDICTION
We have surveyed towards ML, DL, and TL techniques for
predicting the CVD risk. ML algorithms are used to train
for finding data patterns in prediction, classification, and
dimensionality reduction LeCun et al. [30]. ML supports
researchers to developing high-quality diagnostic tools for
healthcare data analysis. The following contributions are
examined by researchers using ML and DL techniques for
CVD prediction. Kanagarathinam et al. [77] developed a
CVD risk prediction model using a CatBoost ML classifier
for CVD classification. Ordikhani et al. [78] implemented
a genetic algorithm to predict CVD risk using the Explain-
able Persian Atherosclerotic CVD Risk Stratification based
on the Isfahan Cohort Study of Iranian adults. Hasan and
Bhattacharjee [79] proposed a method for classifying var-
ious CVD using a multi-dimensional deep CNN network
that utilizes a revised ECG signal to predict CVD risk.
Xia et al. [80] proposed as novel approach for Atrial Fibril-
lation (AF) detection. This technique utilizes the MIT-BIH
with ECG intervals of 5 seconds, which also offers a rapid,
accurate, and efficient tool for AF detection. Samuel et al.
[81] developed a clinical decision support platform using a
hybrid technique of Artificial Neural Networks and Fuzzy
Analytic Hierarchy Process. Yildirim et al. [82] Proposed
DNN model using 12-lead ECG signals to observe vari-
ous rhythm classes from the cardiac arrhythmia. Paul and
Karn [83] used scaled conjugate gradient backpropagation of
the ANN approach to identify cardiac risk. Kuila et al. [84]
detected cardiac arrhythmia using an ELM-CNN classifier
of a 1D 12-layer neural network to minimize overfitting and
improve the learning rate. Kong et al. [85] proposed tempo-
ral regression network architecture to detect End-Diastole
and End-Systole frames from MRI sequences to monitor
cardiovascular function. Vinay et al. [86] proposed super-
vised and unsupervised Recurrent Neural Network based
Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory machine learning
algorithm, along with Generative Adversarial Networks for
early and automatic detection of cardiovascular diseases
using phonocardiogram signals, which are recordings of heart
sounds. Chen et al. [87] developed a segmentation approach
in neural networks to evaluate the structure and performance
of the heart using cardiac MR images.
The K-means clustering algorithm, along with its varia-
tions such as Lloyd’s approach, is used to eliminate depen-
dencies and enhance the quality of clusters Zhang et al. [42].
Hinchcliff et al. [88] used unsupervised model-based cluster-
ing to investigate the role of the heart in systemic sclerosis,
whereas Verma et al. [89] applied clustering techniques
for cardiac disease. ECG image categorization and CVD
detection have been investigated using quantum deep learn-
ing techniques such as Quanvolutional Neural Networks,
quantum-behaved particle swarm optimization, and sup-
port vector machines Prabhu et al. [90]; Elsedimy et al.

explainability of the model’s results SHapley Additive exPla-

of each parameter in prediction. Bhatt et al. [68] proposed
a real-time CVD prediction system using Federated Learn-
ing (FL) and an ANN model, trained on real stroke case
data. Yildirim et al. [82] proposed a 1D-CNN approach for
fast arrhythmia classification using short ECG fragments
(MIT-BIH data). Degirmenci et al. [93] developed a DL
method using images derived from ECG signals to classify
arrhythmias, including normal, PVC, LBBB, RBBB, and
PB. Terrada et al. [94] investigated 835 clinical records of
atherosclerosis patients from three data sources to design a
medical diagnostic support system using ANN for improved
CVD detection.
Tarroni et al. [52] developed a hybrid decision forest
method to analyze short-axis cardiac MR image stacks.
Dutta et al. [95] proposed CNN architecture for correctly
classifying 77% of CHD patients and 81.8% of non-CHD
cases in the testing data. Overall, the model achieves 85.7%
accuracy in classifying the NHANES dataset to predict
coronary heart disease. Ahmed et al. [96] used various tech-
niques such as K nearest neighbour, support vector machine,
multinomial naïve bayes, and gaussian naïve bayes attains
maximum of 67.2% accuracy in CVD prediction. Ensem-

AdaBoost Classifier (ABC) and Random Forest with GBC

racy using an image dataset. Blanchard et al. [98] diagnosed
cardiovascular event risks during sleep apnea using the CNN
model. The result shows well in AUC of 0.82 and sensitivity
of 0.89. Ahmad et al. [99] predicted CVD using the hybrid
CNN+BiLSTM technique with maximum accuracy.
Pan et al. [100] developed Enhanced Deep CNN for

ical datasets such as the Cleveland, Hungarian, Z-Alizadeh

IX. CONCLUSION
Cardiovascular disease poses a significant challenge for indi-
viduals and governing bodies striving to eradicate it. The
survey analyzed the gaps and obstacles in predicting CVD.
Accurate prediction of CVD is crucial for taking proac-
tive measures. Several machine learning, deep learning, and
transfer learning models have been examined, along with
an exploration of optimization techniques. The survey com-
pared the performance metrics of the existing approaches for
CVD prediction. The survey indicates that ML and DL mod-
els achieve accuracy rates between 70% and 100%, where
pre-trained transfer learning models show maximum valida-
tion accuracy (99.9%), sensitivity (96%), specificity (99%),
F1-Score (90%), precision (95%), and
AUC (0.98). The study revealed that ML, DL, TL, and
hybrid models integrating significantly enhance cardiovascu-
lar disease prediction accuracy. Since traditional models face
many challenges, such as high cost, invasiveness.
Contemporary models provide a better solution in CVD
predictions using clinical and imaging data. Deep transfer
learning improves plaque identification and categorization,
leading to better disease staging. The integration of AI and
surgical robots will further assist traditional medicine.
Furthermore, the survey can be extended to analyzing deep
hybrid models incorporating bio-inspired optimization tech-
niques to enhance the prediction accuracy. Future research
in cardiovascular disease prediction should focus on expand-
ing dataset diversity to enhance model generalizability, and
improving interpretability will assist physicians in under-
standing model decisions. Integrating real-time data from
wearable devices may enhance the accuracy and prompt-
ness of cardiovascular disease forecasts. Explainable AI,
attention mechanisms, transformer models, knowledge dis-
tillation, and digital twins are powerful AI techniques with
significant potential in healthcare. Multimodal AI, integrating
cardiac CT, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR),
Genome-wide association studie (GWAS), and genomic AI,
will further enhance the capabilities of AI in cardiac imaging,
leading to more accurate diagnosis and personalized treat-
ment plans.
