Abstract: Cardiovascular disease continues to be a 
predominant cause of death globally, highlighting the necessity 
for effective and proactive diagnostic measures. This research 
presents an AI-driven predictive model connected with the 
Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) for binary and multiclass 
classification of cardiac disorders. The framework initiates with 
meticulous data preprocessing to address absent values and 
standardize features. A deep learning model is developed for 
precise categorization, and the system is enhanced to facilitate 
real-time health monitoring with IoMT-enabled devices. The 
efficacy of the proposed model is assessed utilizing critical 
metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and latency. 
Experimental findings illustrate the model's efficacy in 
identifying cardiac problems and its capability for real-time, 
remote diagnosis. Proposed future additions aim to optimize 
deep learning performance, implement the system on edge 
devices, and facilitate real-time alerting systems. 
Keywords—Heart Disease Prediction, Binary Classification, 
Multiclass Classification, Internet of Medical Things (IoMT), 
Deep Learning, Real-Time Monitoring, Health Informatics 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Cardiovascular disease is a primary cause of morbidity and 
mortality globally, imposing a significant strain on healthcare 
systems. The World Health Organization reports that 
cardiovascular diseases (CVD) account for roughly 32% of 
global mortality, with prevalence rising in conjunction with 
age, lifestyle factors, and comorbidities including diabetes and 
hypertension [1]. Heart illness presents in multiple forms, 
such as coronary artery disease, arrhythmias, and heart failure, 
frequently necessitating intricate clinical management and 
substantial healthcare expenditures. The prognosis for 
individuals with heart illness is often unfavorable; research 
suggests that over fifty percent of those diagnosed with heart 
failure may not survive beyond five years from their initial 
diagnosis. Thus, effective and prompt intervention techniques 
are essential for decreasing mortality rates linked to cardiac 
disease [3]. 
The prompt identification of cardiovascular illness is 
crucial, however it is beset with difficulties. Conventional 
diagnostic techniques, including electrocardiograms (ECGs) 
and stress tests, may fail to detect transient cardiac 
irregularities because of their sporadic nature [4]. Moreover, 
patients may not identify the initial warning signals of heart 
disease, resulting in postponed presentations in clinical 
environments [5]. Ongoing surveillance of vital signs, 
including heart rate and rhythm, can enable prompt 
intervention; yet, attaining real-time analysis presents a 
considerable technical challenge [6]. Contemporary methods 
frequently fail to incorporate advanced monitoring technology 
that provide thorough patient management, particularly in 
remote or resource-constrained environments [7]. 
Recent breakthroughs in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and 
the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) provide promising 
answers for the issues associated with heart disease 
monitoring and prediction. Artificial intelligence systems, 
especially those employing machine learning, have been 
shown to enhance diagnostic precision and decision-making 
support in healthcare environments [1]. Through the analysis 
of extensive datasets, AI can discern patterns that may not be 
readily apparent to healthcare providers, hence improving the 
detection rates of various cardiac diseases [8]. The 
incorporation of IoMT equipment, including wearable sensors 
and mobile ECG monitors, facilitates continuous data 
collection and prompt analysis [9]. The integration of AI with 
IoMT enables proactive healthcare and has the potential to 
revolutionize patient management strategies, resulting in 
enhanced results for individuals with heart disease [10][11]. 
This project seeks to create an AI-driven predictive model 
specifically designed for the classification of cardiac disorders 
in both binary and multiclass contexts. The main objectives 
are: (1) utilizing sophisticated machine learning algorithms to 
accurately classify heart diseases; (2) amalgamating diverse 
patient data to improve predictive precision; and (3) 
implementing the model within an IoMT framework that 
enables real-time monitoring and intervention. This research 
contributes to the creation of a robust predictive model and 
establishes a complete method that integrates AI and IoMT 
technologies to effectively control heart disease. 
The subsequent sections of this work are structured as 
follows: Section II examines pertinent literature on heart 
disease prediction utilizing artificial intelligence and Internet 
of Medical Things frameworks. Section III delineates the 
suggested technique, encompassing data preparation, model 
architecture for binary and multiclass classification, and 
integration with IoMT for real-time health monitoring. 
Section IV examines the outcomes derived from performance 
indicators like accuracy, precision, recall, and latency. 
Ultimately, Section V finishes the study by presenting major 
findings and delineating prospective improvements, including 
deep learning optimization, edge deployment, and real-time 
alerting.

III. 
METHODOLOGY 
The approach that was used to construct the AI-based 
predictive model for binary and multiclass cardiac disease 
categorization and its integration into an Internet of Medical 
Things (IoMT) framework for real-time health monitoring is 
detailed in this part. 
A. Data Collection and Preprocessing 
1) Source of Dataset(s): 
The work makes use of open-source heart illness datasets, 
such as the UCI Machine Learning Repository's Cleveland 
Heart illness dataset and other aggregated datasets that contain 
various variables related to cardiovascular diseases. Both 
binary (illness presence or absence) and multiclass (disease 
severity levels) classification tasks can be accomplished with 
the labeled samples provided by these datasets. 
2) Data Cleaning and Normalization: 
Raw datasets frequently contain redundant, noisy, or 
incorrectly formed entries. A preprocessing pipeline was 
established to eliminate duplicate entries, filter outliers by z-
score analysis, and standardize feature formats. Continuous 
numerical variables, including cholesterol levels, blood 
pressure, and maximal heart rate, were standardized by Min-
Max scaling to standardize them into a 0–1 range, so ensuring 
consistency among input characteristics. 
3) Handling of Missing or Inconsistent Values: 
Missing values in essential characteristics were resolved 
by domain-specific imputation techniques. Numerical fields 
were imputed utilizing median or mean values based on 
distribution, whereas categorical variables were populated 
using the mode. Samples exhibiting above 30% missing

B. Model Architecture 
1) Description of Models Used: 
The proposed approach utilizes a deep learning 
architecture grounded in a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) due 
to its simplicity and efficacy in medical classification tasks. 
The model has input, hidden, and output layers, utilizing 
ReLU activation in the hidden layers and suitable output 
functions (sigmoid or softmax) according to the categorization 
type. 
2) Design for Binary Classification: 
A binary classification model was developed with a single 
output neuron and sigmoid activation to distinguish between 
healthy individuals and those affected by heart disease. The 
loss function utilized was Binary Cross-Entropy, while the 
optimizer chosen for convergence was Adam. The network 
was calibrated to optimize precision and recall because of the 
medical significance of false negatives. 
3) Design for Multiclass Classification: 
Several neurons representing various degrees of heart 
disease severity (e.g., no disease, mild, moderate, severe) were 
present in the output layer of the multiclass configuration. The 
output layer utilizes Categorical Cross-Entropy loss and 
Softmax activation. Class labels were transformed into one-
hot vectors by the process of label encoding. 
4) Training and Validation Strategy: 
Part of the dataset was reserved for training purposes, 
while the other half was used for validation and testing. All 
strata were evenly represented in the final tally thanks to 
stratified sampling. Additionally, the model's generalizability 
was assessed using k-fold cross-validation (k=5). We 
monitored performance metrics throughout epochs to make 
sure we were neither overfitting or underfitting. When 
validation loss occurred, we stopped the process early.

C. IoMT Integration 
1) Framework for Real-Time Data Collection: 
The suggested model is designed to work with an IoMT 
architecture that includes wearable sensors that can take 
readings of vital signs including oxygen saturation, heart rate, 
blood pressure, and electrocardiogram (ECG) in order to 
allow for continuous monitoring in real time. Smart 
wearables, such as fitness bands or chest straps, incorporate 
these sensors, which constantly send physiological signals. 
2) Communication 
Protocols 
and 
Cloud-Based 
Processing: 
Lightweight and dependable communication protocols 
like MQTT or Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) allow for energy-
efficient, low-latency data transmission from sensors to the 
cloud infrastructure. Data is received, preprocessed, and the 
trained AI model is invoked for real-time classification on the 
cloud side by a secure processing layer. 
3) System Architecture for Continuous Monitoring: 
The complete architecture follows a layered design: 
• 
The Device Layer consists of Internet of Medical 
Things (IoMT) wearables that collect data. The Edge 
Layer is an optional intermediate gateway that does 
light analytics and buffering. 
• 
The AI models and categorization tasks are hosted by 
the cloud layer. 
• 
Dashboards and mobile applications offer real-time 
notifications to both patients and doctors at the 
application layer. 
The continuous data flow enables proactive detection of 
heart anomalies, reducing the need for manual intervention 
and supporting timely medical responses.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Here, we detail the experimental results of testing the 
suggested AI-based prediction model for binary and 
multiclass cardiac illness classification, as well as its 
incorporation into an IoMT framework for continuous health 
care tracking. We used industry-standard categorization 
measures and indicators of system-level responsiveness to 
review the model's performance. 
A. Evaluation Metrics 
Five critical performance metrics latency, accuracy, 
precision, recall, and F1-score were used to assess the model. 
In contrast to accuracy, which evaluates the model's general 
correctness, precision and recall zero in on the model's 
capacity to accurately identify ill patients. By measuring the 
ratio of false positives to false negatives, the F1-score offers a 
balanced measure of accuracy and recall. To evaluate real-
time responsiveness in an IoMT context, latency the time it 
takes to get from data input to result output is essential. 
The results obtained by the binary classification model 
were as follows: 94.6% accuracy, 93.2% precision, 95.8% 
recall, and 94.5 F1-score. With class-wise F1-scores 
demonstrating consistent performance across all illness 
severity levels, the multiclass model achieved a slightly lower 
accuracy of 91.3%. 
B. Confusion Matrices for Binary and Multiclass Models 
The results of the classification could be better understood 
visually with the use of confusion matrices. There was 
excellent diagnostic performance in the binary model, as the 
number of correct predictions (both positive and negative) 
greatly exceeded the number of incorrect predictions. As 
anticipated given the overlap in symptoms and feature 
distributions, the multiclass confusion matrix revealed a small 
amount of misclassification between moderate and severe 
cases. Despite this, the model was able to keep all categories' 
prediction rates at a clinically acceptable level. 
C. Comparison with Existing Methods 
Support Vector Machines (SVMs), Decision Trees, and k-
Nearest Neighbors (k-NNs) were some of the more 
conventional machine learning techniques measured against 
the suggested model. Using every metric for evaluation, it 
fared better than these models. For instance, k-NN exhibited 
inadequate scalability in multiclass situations, and the SVM 
model only managed 86.2% accuracy in binary classification. 
When used in conjunction with the IoMT pipeline, the deep 
learning-based MLP model suggested here showed much 
better generalization. 
D. Analysis of IoMT Latency and Real-Time 
Responsiveness 
With an average end-to-end latency of 2.3 seconds, we can 
confidently say that this system meets all the requirements for 
use in real-time health monitoring. Lightweight model 
inference on cloud infrastructure and efficient communication 
protocols like MQTT are responsible for this reduced latency. 
The system proved to be resilient for continuous patient 
monitoring, as the latency remained below 3.1 seconds even 
when subjected to simulated network congestion.

V. 
CONCLUSION 
This research introduced a model for classifying heart 
diseases using artificial intelligence (AI). The model was 
linked into an IoMT framework to allow for real-time 
monitoring and early diagnosis. On benchmark datasets, the 
model showed great recall, accuracy, and precision; it also 
works with multiclass classification tasks. The suggested 
system facilitates proactive cardiac care by acquiring health 
data continuously, analysing it quickly, and notifying users in 
real-time through the use of wearable sensors and fast cloud 
communication 
protocols. 
In 
particular, 
the 
model 
outperforms conventional machine learning methods in terms 
of classification accuracy and system latency, according to the 
experimental findings. By incorporating IoMT, the system 
becomes more applicable to real-life healthcare environments, 
enabling automated, scalable, and remote patient monitoring. 
To enhance clinical decision-making and patient outcomes, 
future work will centre on optimizing the deep learning 
architecture for edge device deployment, implementing 
patient history-based tailored modelling, and incorporating 
real-time feedback mechanisms.
