Abstract— heart disease remains the leading cause of 
mortality worldwide, accounting for millions of deaths 
annually. Despite medical advancements, the early detection 
and accurate prediction of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) 
remain critical challenges. This study explores the application 
of Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) 
techniques in predicting heart disease using clinical data, 
medical images, and physiological signals. Traditional ML 
models, including Decision Trees, Support Vector Machines 
(SVM), Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting (XGBoost, 
LightGBM), are evaluated alongside advanced DL models 
such as Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Convolutional 
Neural Networks (CNN), and Recurrent Neural Networks 
(RNN). A hybrid CNN + LSTM model is proposed to enhance 
predictive accuracy by leveraging both spatial and sequential 
data analysis. The research methodology involves data 
preprocessing, feature selection, model implementation, and 
comparative evaluation using performance metrics such as 
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and AUC-ROC. 
Experimental results indicate that deep learning models, 
particularly CNN and hybrid models, outperform traditional 
ML approaches in heart disease prediction. Further research 
is needed to improve interpretability and real-time 
deployment of AI-driven approaches in clinical settings to 
improve early cardiovascular disease detection and risk 
assessment. 
Keywords— Heart Disease Prediction, Machine Learning, 
Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), 
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN),Hybrid CNN-LSTM.

IV. 
RESEARCH METHODOLOGY 
The study adopts a systematic method of heart disease 
predictive models assessment through machine learning 
(ML) and deep learning (DL) methods. The study is 
centered on data collection, preprocessing, feature 
extraction, model deployment, performance assessment, 
and comparison for the purpose of determining the better 
predictive model. The research study provides a strict 
framework to enhance accuracy, validity, and utility in 
clinical decisions. 
❖ Data Collection 
The data used in this research involve clinical records, 
medical images, and physiological signal data collected 
from public medical repositories and hospital databases. 
The data contain important parameters such as age, 
cholesterol, blood pressure, ECG, and lifestyle. Ethical 
issues and data protection acts were maintained while 
collecting the data. 
❖ Data Preprocessing 
For improving model performance, data preprocessing 
methods were used, such as missing value handling,

to provide class-balanced representation of the dataset. 
❖ Feature Selection 
Feature selection proves to be of significant benefit to 
enhance interpretability and model efficiency. Top

correlation analysis, mutual information, and Recursive

❖ Performance Evaluation 
Model performance was compared against five most 
important metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, 
and AUC-ROC. Cross-validation was used for stability and 
overfitting prevention. Comparison was made to find the 
best-performing models per dataset and modality. 
❖ Comparative Analysis 
ML models were evaluated based on how well they 
perform in handling structured tabular data, whereas DL 
models were evaluated on how well they can handle 
unstructured data such as ECG signals. Hybrid CNN + 
LSTM model demonstrated optimal performance by 
leveraging both feature extraction and sequential data 
processing. Ensemble learning approaches and stacking 
models also improved predictive precision. 
❖ Challenges and Future Directions 
The work recognizes limitations including dataset 
limitation, model explainability, and issues with real-time 
deployment. Explainable AI methods, federated learning 
for privacy-preserving prediction, and real-time clinical 
deployment are avenues to be pursued in order to make 
future work in developing models for predicting heart 
disease more deployable. 
This study design is introducing a structured method 
of developing and validating heart disease prediction 
models. Through the combination of ML and DL 
methodologies, feature optimization, and model validation, 
the research will be advancing precise and reliable 
cardiovascular risk prediction. Model interpretability and 
real-world applicability need to be improved in future 
studies to facilitate clinical decision-making.

VII. 
CONCLUSIONS 
Heart disease remains a significant global health 
concern, necessitating advanced predictive models for 
early diagnosis and prevention. This study demonstrates 
that both Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) 
techniques play a vital role in enhancing heart disease 
prediction through clinical datasets, medical imaging, and 
physiological signals. Traditional ML models, including 
Random Forest, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and 
XGBoost, are efficient for structured data analysis, while 
DL models such as CNN and RNN provide superior 
performance in analyzing ECG signals and medical 
images. The Hybrid CNN + LSTM model outperforms 
individual models by combining spatial feature extraction 
with sequential data processing, leading to higher 
predictive accuracy.
