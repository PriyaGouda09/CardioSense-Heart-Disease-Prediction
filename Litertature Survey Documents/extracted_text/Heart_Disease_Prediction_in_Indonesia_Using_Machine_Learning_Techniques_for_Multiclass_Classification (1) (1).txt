Abstract—This research concentrates on utilizing multiclass 
classification methodology for heart disease prediction. 
Specifically, this paper explores the utilization of Support 
Vector Machine (SVM) and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) 
classifiers to classify various cardiac conditions. This research is 
used to get the best results in predicting heart disease that has a 
classification of more than two categories using machine 
learning methods. The main goal is to contribute insights to 
medical diagnostics by explaining the effectiveness of SVM and 
MLP in improving the accuracy of heart disease prediction 
models in various classes using several factors that influence 
heart disease. The data used in this research has missing values, 
therefore in this research, several methods were used to 
overcome missing values. Determining the best model is done by 
comparing precision, recall, and accuracy values. The best 
model in this research is the SVM model because it has the 
highest accuracy, precision, and recall. It is hoped that this 
research can provide knowledge to optimize its application in 
the identification and classification of heart disease to make it 
more efficient. 
Keywords—heart disease, multiclass, SVM, MLP Classifier, 
classification 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Heart disease is still one of the biggest global health issues 
and causes of mortality. In low- and middle-income nations, 
such as Indonesia, the most populous nation in Southeast 
Asia and home to the fourth biggest population in the world, 
the number keeps rising. Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is 
one of the leading causes of premature mortality in the area, 
encompassing stroke and coronary heart disease (CHD). 
Growing life expectancy, urbanization, demographic shifts, 
epidemiological transition, and the high prevalence of CVD 
risk factors are all contributing reasons to the rise in CVD 
incidence and risk factors in emerging nations like Indonesia 
[1]. Heart failure-related fatalities are becoming more 
commonplace daily. 32 percent of all fatalities worldwide in 
2019 were attributable to cardiovascular illnesses, which 
claimed 17.9 million lives, according to a WHO report. Heart 
attacks and strokes accounted for 85% of these fatalities. The 
cause of this is that individuals do not know why they have 
coronary heart disease [2].  
Coronary heart disease can be caused by a variety of 
causes, including high blood pressure, smoking, diabetes, 
high cholesterol, and other conditions. Smoking is growing 
more and more frequent among young people and the elderly. 
Smoking raises blood stress and constricts the coronary 
heart's arteries, which results in irregular heartbeats. 
Numerous problems are caused by high blood pressure. It 
also causes the lower-left coronary heart chamber to thicken, 
increasing the risk of coronary heart failure. These two 
effects drive the coronary heart to pump blood to the body 
harder [3]. To classify diseases, healthcare analytics which is 
now

methods might not be as accurate and effective as they could 
be, which would need researching more sophisticated

The research of a human disease prediction model based 
on the Random Forest algorithm has been proposed [5]. The 
model is expected to be able to predict possible diseases 
based on the symptoms entered by the user. The model itself 
used Random Forest, Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM), 
and Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithm and a dataset 
from Kaggle in order to train the data. The result of this 
proposed model managed to produce a 97% accuracy in 
predicting possible diseases compared to older models [5]. 
The fields of disease populations, disease status, immune 
response, and health emergencies have all advanced 
significantly as a result of recent advances in artificial 
intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) technologies. 
Though there is still doubt about the usefulness of ML-based 
techniques in practice and how to interpret their findings, 
their use in healthcare settings is growing quickly [6].

In the pursuit of enhancing the accuracy of heart disease 
prediction, this study explores the implementation of 
multiclass classification techniques. As heart diseases 
manifest in various forms, accurate classification is imperative 
for effective medical decision-making. Traditional binary 
classification models often fall short of addressing the 
nuanced complexity of multiclass scenarios. Hence, the focus 
on multiclass classification aims to overcome these limitations 
and provide a more comprehensive framework for predicting 
a spectrum of heart-related ailments. This paper investigates 
the application of two powerful machine learning classifiers: 
Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Multi-Layer Perceptron 
(MLP). The objective is to find the best method to 
systematically categorize diverse types of heart conditions, 
enabling a nuanced understanding of the intricate patterns and 
relationships within heart disease data. 
In this research, classification modeling will be carried out 
on heart disease patients using a multiclass classification 
approach, considering that heart disease conditions can be 
categorized into several levels of severity, such as normal, 
mild (Level 1), moderate (Level 2), and severe (Level 3). Two 
supervised learning methods, namely Support Vector 
Machine (SVM) and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), were 
chosen because each has advantages in handling complex and 
nonlinear data classification. The analysis will focus on 
comparing the performance of the two algorithms. Evaluation 
of model performance will be carried out using various 
metrics that are suitable for multiclass classification, such as 
accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Thus, this research 
is expected to contribute to the development of decision 
support systems in the health sector, especially in data-based 
early diagnosis of heart disease.

III. SUPPORT VECTOR MACHINE 
One of the supervised learning methods that can be used 
for classification and regression is the Support Vector 
Machine (SVM). The concept of SVM is to maximize the 
geometric margin to minimize the empirical classification 
error. Thus, another term for the SVM is the Maximum 
Margin Classifier. The basis of SVM is structural risk 
minimization (SRM). SVM creates a maximal separating 
hyperplane by mapping the input vector to a higher 
dimensional space. Two parallel hyperplanes are constructed 
on either side of the hyperplane that splits the data. The 
separating hyperplane is the one that maximizes the distance 
between the two parallel hyperplanes. The more margin 
between these parallel hyperplanes, the better the 
generalization error of the classifier [11]. 
SVM has been widely implemented in Health Data 
Analysis. One of them is applied to the automatic 
classification of microarray gene expression profiles. 
Theoretically, SVM can examine gene expression profiles 
obtained from tumor samples [12]. 
Class classification problems were primarily supported by 
vector machines, however as computer, network, and 
database technologies advanced quickly and became more 
sophisticated, making it possible to classify and manage vast 
amounts of information, class classification problems were 
unable to keep up with societal demands. The multi-class 
classification issue is extended to use this strategy. The two 
categories of multi-class classification problems in the

class classification issues, first construct a sequence of

final class classification problem is based on two multi-class 
SVM classification methods, in essence. Secondly, create a 
single multi-class support vector machine (SVM) and use it 
to solve the multi-class classification problem [13]. Equation

IV. MULTILAYER PERCEPTRON (MLP) CLASSIFIER 
One of the main focuses of neural network research is 
choosing the appropriate neural network architecture to 
address a certain issue. One or more hidden layers make up 
the multilayer perceptron (MLP). The convergence of MLP 
training methods is significantly influenced by the number of 
neurons in the hidden layers and the initial weights [18]. The 
Feed Forward Neural Network (FFNN) architecture is one 
type of artificial neural network. The FFNN architecture is 
used by the MLP Classifier. This classifier learns to categorize 
with a multilayer perceptron using backpropagation. The input 
layer, hidden layer, and output layer are the three layers that 
make up the MLP Classifier [19]. Both the input layer and the 
hidden layer have several nodes. The number of nodes in the 
input layer is an independent variable. On the other hand, the 
hidden layer could have one or more layers and nodes [20]. 
Processing from the input layer to the hidden layer and then to 
the output layer is called forward propagation. Then the 
activation function value is propagated to the next layer. The 
next layer can be another hidden layer or an output layer. MLP 
is trained from training data through processing which is 
called backpropagation. This process can be described as a 
way to improve errors [21]. Fig. 2 shows the architecture of 
FFNN. Meanwhile, the general model of MLP is written in

V. DATA AND METHODS 
The data used in this paper was obtained from one of the 
hospitals in Indonesia. The samples in this research were 
patients at the hospital, whether patients suffered from heart 
disease or not. The number of samples in this research was 
9756. However, the data in this research contained many 
missing values. There are dependent and independent 
variables used in this research. Table 1 shows the independent 
variables and Table 2 shows the levels of dependent variable. 
There are two methods that can be used to carry out 
multiclass classification, namely SVM and MLP [24]. The 
stages of this research are as follows. First, perform Pre-
Processing on the data to overcome missing values. In this 
research, there are two ways to do this, namely filling in 
empty data with median values and deleting empty data. 
Second, carry out analysis using SVM on data that has no 
missing values using four kernel functions. Third, carry out 
analysis using MLP on data that has no missing values using 
three activation functions. Fourth, compare the SVM and 
MLP models to get the best model using accuracy, precision 
and recall values.

VI. DATA PRE-PROCESSING 
Before carrying out classification analysis on heart disease 
patient data, pre-processing is first carried out. This is 
because the data in this research has many missing values. 
Then handling missing data must be done first. There are 
several variables that have missing values. In this research, 
variables that have missing values are imputed using the 
Median value for the numerical variables. This is because 
there are outliers in these variables, then the imputation uses 
the Median value. Numerical variables that were imputed 
using median values included Total Protein, Albumin, 
Globulins, Total Bilirubin, Bilirubin, and Serum Glutamic 
Oxaloacetic Transaminase. Meanwhile, categorical variables 
that have missing values are imputed using the category that 
has the most frequency. The imputed categorical variables 
include Glucose, Cholesterol, Triglycerides, High-density 
lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, and Urea. After that, the data 
is divided into training and testing data with a ratio of 80% 
training data and 20% testing data.

VIII. CONCLUSION 
By carrying out data training experiments on each method 
and using different kernels, the reseach was able to give an 
approximate case on the performances of using both support 
vector machine and multi-layer perceptron classifiers to 
classify specific traits in heart disease patients to each level of 
the disease, which in turn can be a reference for future patient 
diagnosis. Each method used has shown great numbers and is 
an indicator that any of the methods might in fact be used for 
disease predictions in general, not just for heart disease. In this 
case in particular, the research finds that using SVM classifier 
with the radial basis function kernel has a higher accuracy than 
other methods.  
It is advised to conduct further experimentations with 
different datasets to further understand the differences in using 
each of the methods used in the study against datasets with 
different traits. The parameter tuning is done mostly manually 
and might be prone to missing smaller parameters with better 
statistics and as such a more algorithmic solution is advised.  
Better methods in data pre-processing is also advised as to find 
out deeper connection between missing values in data and 
prediction accuracy. 
In the case of heart disease classification, there is an 
imbalance in the amount of data in each class. Further research 
should apply techniques such as SMOTE, class weighting, or 
cost-sensitive learning to improve model performance, 
especially in minority classes. In addition, in the modeling 
analysis to obtain the best model, the combination of Support 
Vector Machine (SVM) and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) 
shows potential in improving classification accuracy 
compared to the single use of each method. Further 
researchers are advised to explore hybrid model architectures.
